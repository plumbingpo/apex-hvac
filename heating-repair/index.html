Warning Signs for Heating Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • Higher heating bills with less comfort to show for them
  • Clicking and failed ignition attempts before the furnace gives up
  • Banging at startup caused by burners lighting late
  • Burning, musty, or rotten-egg odors when the heat runs
  • Cold air, or no air at all, when the thermostat calls for heat
  • Uneven heat, with certain rooms that never warm up
  • An outdoor heat pump coil that stays iced over
  • The system starting and stopping every few minutes without warming the house

Why Hire a Professional for Heating Repair?

Combustion is what makes heating repair different from the rest of home comfort work. Delayed ignition booms unburned gas inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal each time it happens. A cracked exchanger can leak combustion gases into the air your family breathes, and a flue that stops drafting pushes carbon monoxide into the living space instead of outdoors. None of these problems make themselves obvious to someone without training and instruments. Diagnosis has its own traps as well. Short cycling can point to a failing limit switch or to a furnace protecting itself from a clogged filter, and a flame sensor cleaned without finding out why it fouled simply sets up the next lockout.

That is why Apex HVAC does not guess. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate so the blower and the fire are properly matched. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve before blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because your family's warmth and safety depend on it.

Heating Repair Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Replace filters monthly through the heating season to keep airflow strong and the heat exchanger from overheating
  • Keep chlorine products, paints, and solvents away from the furnace, because their fumes corrode burners
  • Book a fall tune-up so worn parts are caught before the first cold night
  • Avoid crowding the furnace with storage, which can starve the burners of air
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
  • Make sure every level has a working carbon monoxide detector, and test it monthly
  • Clear snow, leaves, and overgrown shrubs from around the heat pump so defrost works properly
